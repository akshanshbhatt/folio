# Scripts, performances and creative handoffs

**Design proposal.** A creator should be able to start a production with whatever expresses the piece best. For a comedy that might be a premise. For a dance film it might be a rehearsal video with marks on the floor. Each handoff should tell the next person which decisions the creator has made, where there is room to invent, and what to open or play to understand the difference.

## Choose what leads the edit

| Starting material | What the creator can fix in place | Work that can be delegated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sentence or rough outline | Premise, audience, ending, or one moment the story must earn | Propose beats, competing plots, dialogue and boards. Keep alternatives as drafts. |
| A script with sparse directions | Exact lines, speaker attribution and any specified actions | Work out coverage, blocking, reaction shots and pacing around those choices. |
| A storyboard or timed animatic | Composition, shot order, a gesture or the frame where a cut lands | Build the set, animate the rig, record performances and mix to picture. |
| Recorded footage, dialogue or music | A source take, selected intervals, an intentional pause or a musical downbeat | Find cutaways, draw overlays, prepare captions, mix and package the edit. |

A production can combine all four. A creator might lock a single line and invite a rewrite of the ending. The original text, recording or board should stay available beside any derived transcript or shot plan. Give each reused beat a stable identity so a reordered script can keep its links to performances, evidence and artwork.

## Give the writer a starting shape

A series could supply a suggested structure along with its tone. An investigation might open with a visual puzzle, test a prediction and end on the result. A comedy might open on a character already making a mistake, escalate it and pay it off with a look. A podcast profile might start with a guest's recollection, let the host introduce the guest later, and return to the recording at the close.

A vague brief gives a writer room to propose a hook or introduction. When a creator supplies an opening, preserve it. Mark whether a beat is deliberately omitted, already supplied, or open for drafting. “No introduction this week” resolves the question. “Open on the chipped rim” can fulfill the hook through a picture alone. A beat label belongs in the editing notes unless the creator asks to display it. A silent film can go straight from boards and movement cues to picture and sound design.

## Direct a moment precisely

Imagine a flashback set before a kitchen mug was repaired. The creator supplies a line, “Turn it around,” and a 24 fps animatic. The director chooses the doorway angle. On “around,” the performer rotates the mug. When its chip comes into view, hold on Kit's raised eyebrow for 192 frames. Keep the room tone audible and leave that reaction free of dialogue and music.

The line fixes the words. A recorded take supplies their timing. The animatic fixes the eight-second hold. The animator needs the chipped prop, the character’s eyebrow control and the time of the turn. The mixer needs the protected interval and its room tone. If a replacement take moves “around” later, a phrase-bound turn can follow it. If this would run past a locked cut, show the competing timings and let the director choose a shorter move, a different take or a new cut. Preserve both source time and edit time so the editor can find the exact performance again. Folio's current cue engine already resolves measured phrases and named marks into frame positions. ## Choose the owner at each handoff

- **From a plot.** A creator supplies the ending and delegates research, writing, boards, visual production, sound, thumbnail concepts and assembly. Give the writer the approved evidence and relevant story history. Give the designer the selected visual moment and any spoiler limit. The creator chooses which decisions require review.
- **With a mixed team.** A writer delivers dialogue, a director supplies the animatic, an agent drafts coverage and an animator supplies the finished performance. The editor can accept coverage shot by shot. A revised camera angle should reopen the affected picture and any cover drawn from it.
- **From finished material.** A creator supplies the cut list, footage, audio, artwork and optional captions. Assembly can use source ranges, mix instructions and export settings directly. This route should run with zero model calls. The recorded-conversation example already preserves the source sound across its selected interval.
