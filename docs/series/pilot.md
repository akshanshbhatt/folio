# Two episodes in the same kitchen

The runnable public fixture contains an original kitchen set, cast and sound, two workflow graphs and an editable animatic. It exercises a nine-second repair scene and an eleven-second flashback. Its test decodes the working films and takes a drawing revision through an actual edit. [Render the films](../../examples/kitchen-series/README.md) · [CLI and edition guide](commands.md)

The sections below describe the production exercise. The example animates 2D drawings, the story adapter uses supplied local editorial material, and the editable actor handoff ships with a synthetic scratch take. ## Establish the series

Kit and Rue are siblings who share a kitchen. Their favorite mug has a chip in its rim. The comedy comes from what one sibling carefully avoids saying. The season follows small acts of repair as the household learns to trust one another again. An unresolved subplot concerns the missing spare key. Record which characters know about the key, and whether that information matters to the current scene.

The creator chooses an ordinary kitchen wide shot, a font for end credits, a palette, two cast references and a quiet room-tone recording. The film plays at 24 frames per second. The character animator typically holds poses for two frames while the camera can move on every frame. The kitchen set has a floor plan and a native scene. The mug has a chipped state and a repaired state. A drawing and a rigged model can depict either state. The scratch dialogue track and finished actor performance are separate takes.

## Episode two starts from a plot

The creator writes one sentence: “Kit repairs the mug before Rue comes home and tries to keep the repair a surprise.” They delegate a plot outline, dialogue, shot proposals and thumbnail ideas. The writer receives the season premise, earlier accepted events and examples of the siblings' humor. The director chooses a pause before the reveal. The creator asks an actor for Rue's final line and keeps that take while the animator revises the reaction.

A review should let the creator make two independent decisions. First, accept the film. Second, record the repair as a story event and record the later moment when Rue discovers it. The first shot after the repair can show the joined rim even though Rue cannot yet explain it. A thumbnail that reveals the repair should be easy to substitute if the producer wants to preserve the surprise.

## Episode three starts from a hand-drawn animatic

The opening is a flashback to the day before the repair. The director supplies their board, a doorway shot and an eight-second silent reaction. At 24 fps the hold occupies 192 output frames. Room tone continues. The director gives the animator Kit's raised eyebrow as a fixed pose and delegates the rest of the blocking.

The flashback should resolve to the chipped mug. The writer's brief should reflect what each sibling knew before the repair, even though the preceding release showed it. The camera instruction applies to this scene alone. At the return to the present, the repaired mug becomes available again and the ordinary camera default applies to an otherwise unspecified shot. The final sound mix should have the actor's intended silence and the original room tone. A synthesized line or an automatic silence trim would both change the performance.

## Rehearse a revision

After the first two cuts, the prop artist thickens the mug handle. The producer selects the candidate revision and asks where it is used. The expected report identifies the exact working shots and any cover made from them, the representation each consumes, and the pinned editions that used the earlier version. A rig update and a physical repair are different changes: the updated drawing can still depict a chipped mug.

Adopt the new handle in a single working shot. Compare the old and new frames, inspect the resulting review state, and reopen the previously exported film. If a generator cannot reproduce identical pixels from the same prompt, the original chosen output must still be available to that edition. If a licensed source cannot be packaged, the delivery record should identify what to relink.

## Evidence to inspect

| Exercise | Observable result |
| --- | --- |
| Start a new episode with only the premise | The proposed stage owners, inherited defaults, missing creative choices and supporting references are visible before production begins. |
| Ask why the doorway camera was selected | The answer points to the director's scene instruction and identifies the series default that would apply outside the scene. |
| Inspect writer and animator handoffs | The writer receives the appropriate story-time knowledge. The animator receives the chosen kitchen, mug state, rig controls and performance reference. Each handoff lists what it includes. |
| Try an incompatible asset | A request for a handle rotation against a flattened still exposes the missing control. An artist can supply a rig, revise the shot or use the still as drawn. |
| Check the finished flashback | Sample frames show the chip and the held expression. The time map shows 192 frames. Listening confirms room tone and the absence of a spoken line in that interval. |
| Request another pass after a manual edit | The actor's chosen take and the director's fixed timing remain identifiable. The creator can accept, replace or explicitly return just the selected work to automation. |
| Promote a one-off style choice | The proposed change shows its source, scope and affected episodes. An unpromoted doorway shot leaves future episodes' camera choice alone. |
| Reopen a released edition after a redesign | The delivered movie and archived source digests still identify the approved edition. Later work can opt into the new asset independently. |

## Try the same design outside fiction

For an educational series, give a recurring illustrated guide a raincoat for one field experiment. The next episode should reuse their usual outfit and voice. Updating the guide's outline should identify illustrations that use it. A correction to a scientific claim should travel through the evidence and script handoffs, independently of the costume decision.

For a podcast, supply final footage, an editor's selects and a completed cover. Choose manual ownership for every creative handoff and delegate only assembly and export to local tools. The resulting cut should preserve source order as edited, speakers and intentional pauses. It should run without a model account or an invented script. A later episode can reuse the title treatment and microphone mix while starting from another recording.
